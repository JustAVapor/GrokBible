Bibliography

Texts, Tools, and Commentaries

The works below are the principal texts, reference tools, and commentaries whose scholarship informs this study. Their inclusion does not mean that every view they hold is endorsed; as explained in “How to Use This Study,” every interpretation has been weighed against the text of Scripture itself.

Texts of Scripture

  • Holmes, Michael W., ed. The Greek New Testament: SBL Edition. Atlanta: Society of Biblical Literature; Bellingham, WA: Logos Bible Software, 2010.
  • Novum Testamentum Graece. Nestle-Aland 28th ed. Stuttgart: Deutsche Bibelgesellschaft, 2012.
  • Elliger, K., and W. Rudolph, eds. Biblia Hebraica Stuttgartensia. 5th ed. Stuttgart: Deutsche Bibelgesellschaft, 1997.
  • Westminster Leningrad Codex. Electronic text, with morphology by the Open Scriptures Hebrew Bible project.
  • Rahlfs, Alfred, and Robert Hanhart, eds. Septuaginta. Rev. ed. Stuttgart: Deutsche Bibelgesellschaft, 2006.
  • Tauber, James, ed. MorphGNT: morphological analysis of the SBLGNT. Electronic edition.

Reference Works

  • Bauer, Walter, Frederick W. Danker, W. F. Arndt, and F. W. Gingrich. A Greek-English Lexicon of the New Testament and Other Early Christian Literature. 3rd ed. Chicago: University of Chicago Press, 2000.
  • Beale, G. K., and D. A. Carson, eds. Commentary on the New Testament Use of the Old Testament. Grand Rapids: Baker Academic, 2007. (Hebrews by George H. Guthrie.)
  • Koehler, Ludwig, and Walter Baumgartner. The Hebrew and Aramaic Lexicon of the Old Testament. Leiden: Brill, 1994–2000.
  • Metzger, Bruce M. A Textual Commentary on the Greek New Testament. 2nd ed. Stuttgart: Deutsche Bibelgesellschaft, 1994.
  • Wallace, Daniel B. Greek Grammar beyond the Basics. Grand Rapids: Zondervan, 1996.

Ancient Sources Cited for Background

  • The Dead Sea Scrolls (especially 11QMelchizedek, the Community Rule [1QS], and 4QDeutq).
  • Josephus. Jewish Antiquities; The Jewish War; Against Apion.
  • The Mishnah (especially the tractates Yoma, Tamid, and Middot).
  • Philo of Alexandria. Works.
  • Second Temple Jewish writings: Sirach, Wisdom of Solomon, 1–4 Maccabees, 1 Enoch, Jubilees.
  • Early Christian writings: 1 Clement; Tertullian, On Modesty; Origen and Clement of Alexandria (as reported by Eusebius).
  • Roman writers: Suetonius, Lives of the Caesars; Tacitus, Annals; Cicero, In Defense of Rabirius.
